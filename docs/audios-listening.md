# Audios del Listening (prueba de nivel de inglés)

La prueba ya funciona sin estos archivos: mientras no existan, el navegador lee cada guion con una voz sintética en inglés.
Cuando subas los MP3, la web los usa automáticamente.

## Qué archivo grabar
- Un MP3 por guion, con el nombre exacto que aparece abajo (por ejemplo `l-a2-01.mp3`).
- Inglés británico o estadounidense claro, velocidad natural. Voz de **mujer** y de **hombre** donde lo indica el guion.
- Sin música de fondo. Deja ~1 segundo de silencio al inicio y al final. Calidad 64–128 kbps, mono.
- No grabes las preguntas ni las opciones: solo el guion.

## Dónde ponerlos
Copia los archivos a `public/media/audio/` en el repositorio y publica (`npx wrangler deploy`).
La web los busca en `https://forwinnersec.com/media/audio/<nombre>.mp3`.

## Parte 3 · B2 (entrevistas)

### `l-b2-01.mp3` · A young entrepreneur

**Mujer:** Today I'm talking to Andrés, who started a business selling recycled backpacks when he was only nineteen. Andrés, how did it all begin?

**Hombre:** Honestly, by accident. I needed a bag for university and couldn't afford a new one, so I made one out of old banners. A few classmates asked me to make them one too, and I realised there was a market.

**Mujer:** Was it difficult at first?

**Hombre:** The hardest thing wasn't making the bags; it was learning to manage money. In the first year I sold quite a lot but barely made a profit, because I hadn't calculated my costs properly.

**Mujer:** What advice would you give to young people?

**Hombre:** Don't wait until you have the perfect idea. Start small and make your mistakes early, when they're cheap.

### `l-b2-02.mp3` · Teenagers and sleep

**Hombre:** Many parents believe that teenagers who use their phones at night are simply lazy in the mornings. However, research suggests the problem is more complex. Doctor Vega, can you explain?

**Mujer:** Certainly. During adolescence, the body clock naturally shifts later, so teenagers genuinely feel sleepy later at night. Screens make this worse, because the light delays the release of melatonin, the hormone that helps us fall asleep. So it isn't laziness. It's biology plus habits.

**Hombre:** So what should families do?

**Mujer:** Rather than banning phones completely, which rarely works, I'd suggest agreeing on a time when all devices go into another room. And that includes the parents' devices.

### `l-b2-03.mp3` · Studying online

**Mujer:** I have to say, I didn't expect to enjoy the online course as much as I did.

**Hombre:** Really? I found it hard to stay motivated without a teacher in the room.

**Mujer:** That's true at the beginning, but once I set myself a fixed timetable, it was fine. And I could watch the explanations again whenever I got lost.

**Hombre:** I suppose that's an advantage. What I missed most was chatting with classmates after class.

**Mujer:** Well, I agree that you lose some of the social side. That's why I'd choose a mix: online lessons with a face-to-face session every week.

## Parte 4 · C1 (conferencias y debates)

### `l-c1-01.mp3` · Language and thought

**Mujer:** There's a popular idea that the language we speak determines what we're able to think. In its strong form, this claim has largely been abandoned: speakers of languages without a word for a particular colour can still perceive that colour perfectly well. What researchers do accept, however, is a weaker version: that language can nudge our attention. For example, speakers of languages that describe directions using north, south, east and west, rather than left and right, tend to be remarkably good at keeping track of where they are. So language doesn't imprison thought, but it may well train certain habits of mind.

### `l-c1-02.mp3` · Who benefits from tourism?

**Hombre:** The argument that tourism automatically benefits local communities simply doesn't hold up. In many destinations, most of the money ends up with international hotel chains and tour operators.

**Mujer:** I'd challenge that slightly. It depends enormously on how tourism is managed. In community-based projects in the Amazon, for instance, families own the lodges and decide how many visitors to accept.

**Hombre:** Granted, but those projects are the exception, not the rule. And even there, visitor numbers can creep up once a place becomes fashionable.

**Mujer:** Which is precisely why regulation matters. The problem isn't tourism itself; it's leaving it entirely to the market.
